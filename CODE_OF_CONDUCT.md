# Code of Conduct

We aim to create a professional, respectful learning environment where everyone — experienced or not — can ask questions, make mistakes in public and learn from them.

## Where this applies

Live sessions (offline and online), chats and discussion channels, GitHub Issues and Pull Requests, Capstone team repositories, code reviews, and any other space used for the course.

## What we expect

- **Respect others.** Treat classmates, the trainer and the staff with courtesy, whatever their background, experience level, language or pace.
- **Ask questions.** A question is never "too basic". Asking early is part of the working culture we practise.
- **Help teammates.** Explain, pair, review. Helping someone understand is different from handing over a solution (see [COURSE-RULES.md](COURSE-RULES.md), academic integrity).
- **Be constructive.** Critique the code, the design or the decision — never the person. In code reviews say what is wrong, why, and what you suggest instead.
- **Be honest.** Say what you do not understand. Admit mistakes and fix them. Name trade-offs and risks openly — the course grades honesty about them.
- **Be reliable.** Keep your commitments in team work: reviews within the agreed time, tasks done or flagged early.
- **Protect privacy.** Do not share another person's work, messages, recordings or personal information outside the course without permission. Never post secrets or credentials.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, insults, discrimination, mockery of someone's questions, language or ability
- Intimidation, repeated unwanted contact, or personal attacks in reviews and chats
- Sharing or presenting someone else's work as your own
- Sabotaging a teammate's work or a shared repository
- Disrupting sessions or ignoring a request to stop

## Capstone teamwork

Your team charter (Session 25) states how disagreements are settled. If a team conflict cannot be solved inside the team, bring it to the trainer **early** — a short conversation is always better than a silent gap. Unequal contribution is handled through the git log and the individual grade, not through blame.

## Reporting a concern

Speak to the trainer privately, in person or through the course channel. Reports are treated seriously and with discretion.

## What happens next

The trainer may remind, warn, or — for serious or repeated violations — remove a participant from a session or the course, in line with ITSharks policy.
